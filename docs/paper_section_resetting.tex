\subsection{Resetting collapsed members}\label{sec:reset}

Entropy minimization on a long stream can drive a member into collapse: its accuracy falls to a few percent and does not recover.
The gate of Section~\ref{sec:gate} already shifts weight to the member that still works, but the collapsed member keeps adapting on a degenerate
signal and never becomes useful again. We therefore restore a member to its source weights as soon as a label-free detector says that it has collapsed.
Everything the rule uses is already available: one proxy value per member and per proxy batch, no labels and no extra forward pass.

\paragraph{Signal.}
Every $b$ samples the gate computes the reliability proxy $r_t^{(m)} \in (0,1)$ of each member $m \in \{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{S}\}$ on the $t$-th proxy batch.
The rule reads only the member's own value, on the logit scale,
\begin{equation}
  s_t^{(m)} = \operatorname{logit}\!\big(\operatorname{clip}(r_t^{(m)}, \epsilon, 1-\epsilon)\big),
\end{equation}
so a member is never reset because of what its partner does, and the decisions of the two members are independent.

\paragraph{Detector.}
For one member (we drop the index $m$) the detector compares a fast and a slow exponential moving average of $s_t$,
\begin{equation}
  f_t = (1-\alpha_{\mathrm{f}})\, f_{t-1} + \alpha_{\mathrm{f}}\, s_t, \qquad
  g_t = (1-\alpha_{\mathrm{s}})\, g_{t-1} + \alpha_{\mathrm{s}}\, s_t, \qquad 0 < \alpha_{\mathrm{s}} < \alpha_{\mathrm{f}} < 1,
\end{equation}
and flags the member when the fast average has fallen more than $\delta$ noise units below the slow one,
\begin{equation}
  z_t = \frac{g_t - f_t}{\hat\sigma_{t-1}} > \delta .
\end{equation}
The test is one-sided: a rise of the proxy never triggers it. A sudden collapse moves $f_t$ first, a gradual decay moves $g_t$ later, and in both cases the gap
$g_t - f_t$ opens. $\hat\sigma_t$ is a running standard deviation of $s_t$ (an exponentially weighted variance with a fixed rate), so that $\delta$ is expressed in units of the
member's own noise; the scale used to judge $s_t$ is the one \emph{before} $s_t$ enters the variance, so that a collapse does not inflate its own yardstick.
The first $W_0$ proxy batches after the start, and after every reset, are a warm-up: no member is flagged, both averages start at the warm-up mean and $\hat\sigma$ at the warm-up
standard deviation. The detector costs $O(1)$ per proxy batch.

\paragraph{Action.}
When a member is flagged, its adapted parameters are restored to the source weights and its optimizer state is emptied (momentum buffers set to zero), so that the next
update is the first update of a fresh run; its detector restarts with a new warm-up. The other member, the data stream and the gate are left untouched: the gate keeps
reading both proxies and re-weights the freshly reset member as its proxy recovers. The decisions for a proxy batch are taken for both members before any action is applied,
so both members can be flagged on the same batch.

\paragraph{Per-member settings.}
Different architectures collapse at different speeds, so each member $m$ has its own detector setting
$\mathbf{c}_m = (\alpha_{\mathrm{f}}^{m}, \alpha_{\mathrm{s}}^{m}, W_0^{m}, \delta^{m})$ in addition to its own learning rate $\eta_m$.
Since the rule reads only a member's own proxy and the gate does not feed back into adaptation, a member's trajectory depends only on $(\eta_m, \mathbf{c}_m)$ and on its own
reset times; the two members are therefore tuned independently, each on its own accuracy, on held-out corruptions that do not appear in the test stream
(Appendix~\ref{app:reset_tuning}). The tuned values for our pairs are in Table~\ref{tab:reset_settings}.
