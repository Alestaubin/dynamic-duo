\section*{Summary}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A duo is run in several \emph{modes} (each member alone, the independent pair, one member adapting on the duo's entropy, both adapting on it, with or without a gate, with or without resets). Each mode adapts on a different loss, so each has \textbf{its own learning rates}; none can be borrowed from another mode. Part~I lists what is tuned, how, and why (Sections~\ref{sec:parts}--\ref{sec:borrow}); the shared protocol is Section~\ref{sec:protocol}.
  \item \textbf{Joint DD with the gate is the case where two things must be tuned together:} the gate sits inside the loss, so its \(\beta\) changes what each member learns, and the best rates depend on \(\beta\) (and \(\beta\) on the rates). On the main duo, the pair tuned without the gate drifts as soon as the gate is switched on (55.6\% at \(\beta=0.5\)), while the pair retuned at \(\beta=0.5\) reaches 60.6\%; the best \vit{} rate falls from \lr{3.2}{-3} at \(\beta=0\) to \lr{5.7}{-4} at \(\beta=1\) (Section~\ref{sec:jointdd}, Table~\ref{tab:betapair}). In that search the gate does not help (\(\beta=0\) wins), but this could only be known by retuning.
  \item Part~II gives the tuned rates of the two duos (Tables~\ref{tab:rates_main}--\ref{tab:rates_cnx}), including the continual and reset regimes where they exist.
  \item Part~III is the \vit{} + \cnx{} case. \textbf{On the four tuning shifts, adapting helps:} the duo goes from 50.9\% (frozen) to 55.6--56.0\% in four of the modes, tied within 0.4 points. \textbf{On the 15 test corruptions it does not transfer:} the frozen duo gets 41.0\%; Independent large (44.2\%) and Independent both (42.2\%) are above it, but Large adapt (41.2\%), Joint (38.0\%), Small adapt (33.6\%) and Independent small (20.1\%) are at or below it. \cnx{} adapting on its own entropy falls below 10\% on five of the fifteen corruptions at the independent pair's rate and on ten at its single-model rate.
  \item \textbf{Why the tuning did not see it:} on the tuning shifts \cnx{}'s average is flat across rates because gains on three shifts offset a collapse on one (Gaussian blur, 20\% frozen to 2\%); the criterion accepts that trade, and the tuning shifts contain none of the corruptions that break it on the test set (fog, snow, frost, elastic, contrast, zoom blur). No selection was changed after seeing test numbers; whether to adopt a rule that sees single-shift collapses is open (Section~\ref{sec:open}).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Part I. What has to be tuned, and why}

\section{What a run consists of}\label{sec:parts}
\paragraph{The duo.} A large and a small member (here \vit{} with \rn{} or \cnx{}; \vit{} and \cnx{} use LayerNorm, \rn{} BatchNorm), adapted with Tent: entropy minimization of the normalization layers' affine parameters, SGD with momentum 0.9, one step per batch of 64. Their logits are combined into one prediction.

\paragraph{The static duo and the gate.} The \emph{static duo} combines the members with fixed temperatures, \(\hat z = \tfrac12\,(z_L/T_L + z_S/T_S)\). A \emph{gate} replaces the equal weights by \(w_L = \sigma(\beta\,(x_L - x_S))\) and \(w_S = 1-w_L\), where \(x_L, x_S\) are label-free reliability scores (a proxy, here the normalized nuclear norm of the member's predictions over proxy batches of 256 images, put on the logit scale). \(\beta = 0\) gives \(w_L = w_S = \tfrac12\), the static duo.

\paragraph{Two ways to use the gate.} In \textbf{Vanilla DD} each member adapts on its \emph{own} entropy and the gate only combines their predictions at the output: the gate is outside the loss, so it changes the duo's prediction but not what the members learn. In \textbf{Joint DD} both members adapt on the entropy of the \emph{gated} prediction: the gate is inside the loss, so \(\beta\) changes what each member learns.

\paragraph{Modes.} Table~\ref{tab:modes_def} lists the ways of adapting that the results compare, with the signal that drives the members. ``Joint'' without ``DD'' denotes the static duo (\(\beta=0\)) in the loss; this is the Joint row of script 03 and of Part~III.

\paragraph{Two settings.} \emph{Episodic}: every stream (one corruption) starts from the source weights, the members are reset before each corruption. \emph{Continual}: one long stream through several shifts with no reset, or with resets triggered by a detector (the \emph{resetting} experiments). The episodic modes are tuned in Sections~\ref{sec:each} (first seven subsections); the continual and reset regimes need their own tuning (Section~\ref{sec:reset}).

\paragraph{Streams.} \emph{Tuning shifts} (the only data any choice is made on): ImageNet-C speckle noise, Gaussian blur, spatter and saturate at severity 5, 50,000 images each. \emph{Test corruptions}: the 15 standard ImageNet-C corruptions at severity 5; none of the tuning shifts is among them. Accuracy is the online accuracy of the pre-update predictions; ``Avg'' is the mean over the corruptions of a table; \(\pm\) is the std over stream-order seeds.

\section{The tuning protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
One protocol is used for every item, so that the rows of a table differ only in how the members adapt:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tuning shifts only.} Nothing is chosen on a test corruption or on any stream built from one. The test stream is run once, with the frozen choices.
  \item \textbf{Temperatures first and fixed.} \(T_L, T_S\) are fitted once on the \emph{frozen} members' logits (not refitted per mode or per rate), so a rate is never tuned against a temperature that moves with it.
  \item \textbf{Rate only.} SGD with momentum 0.9, batch 64, one step; only the learning rate is searched, on a grid of ratio \(\sqrt2\) (a coarser \(\times2\) ladder first), extended until the optimum is interior, anchored on the member's single-model rate for the duo modes (``individual rates first, duos from them'').
  \item \textbf{Criterion.} The whole-stream accuracy of the thing the mode outputs (the member's own for a single model, the static or gated duo's otherwise), the mean over the four tuning shifts. The worst stream is reported but does not decide. (Since 3 November the \vit{} + \cnx{} duo uses the \rl{worst\_shift} criterion instead, in every tuning and test: among the candidates within 1 point of the best mean, the best worst shift; Section~\ref{sec:open}. The main duo keeps the mean. Parts~II and III report the mean-criterion rates unless stated.)
  \item \textbf{Late-stream guard.} A candidate whose accuracy on the last 20\% of each stream (mean over the shifts) is below the frozen reference's (the frozen member, or the frozen duo for a pair or duo-entropy mode) is ruled out as drifting.
  \item \textbf{Interior optimum.} A winner on the edge of its grid is not recorded; the grid is extended.
  \item \textbf{Seeds.} The search runs on one stream-order seed; the finalists (the leading few) run on seeds 0, 1, 2 and the final choice is made on the three.
\end{itemize}

\section{How each mode is tuned, and why it has to be}\label{sec:each}
For each item: how it is tuned, why it cannot be taken from another, and, where we have one, a number.

\subsection{Temperatures}
Fitted per duo on the frozen members' logits over the pooled tuning shifts (\rl{checkpoints/fixed\_ts/}; main duo \(T_L = 0.651\), \(T_S = 1.0097\); \vit{} + \cnx{} \(T_L = 0.7157\), \(T_S = 0.9416\)). They depend on the members' logit scales, hence per duo; they are held fixed for every mode and every tuning below.

\subsection{Single model (Independent large / small), 02c}
Each member alone, Tent on its own entropy, its own accuracy on the four shifts; the rate must be interior and is then kept. These rates anchor every later grid, and are the rows ``Independent large / small''. They are \emph{not} what a member needs inside a duo (below).

\subsection{Independent pair (Independent both, Vanilla DD's members), 02c}
Both members adapt on their own entropy, scored by the \emph{duo}: a member's best rate alone is not necessarily best in the combination, because the duo's accuracy depends on both members' errors. For \vit{} + \cnx{} the two differ by a factor of eleven for the \cnx{} (single \lr{3.5}{-5}, pair \lr{3.1}{-6}): at the single rates the duo gets 52.3\%, at the pair 55.6\% (why: Section~\ref{sec:whypair}). For the main duo they coincide (\lr{7}{-5}, \lr{5}{-4}). Because the members do not see each other's loss, their logits can be computed independently and every pair is scored from stored logits (a \(n\times n\) grid from \(n\) runs), which is why this is the cheap case.

\subsection{Large adapt and Small adapt, 02d and 02e}
One member adapts on the static duo's entropy while the other is frozen, so the member's loss is the entropy of a combination with a constant partner: a different loss, a different best rate. A one-dimensional grid from 0.5 to 64 times the single rate (extended downward when the optimum is at the edge: to 0.125 for the \cnx{}), finalists on three seeds. The numbers: on the main duo the rates are \lr{1.12}{-3} (\vit{}) and \lr{1.4}{-3} (\rn{}), against \lr{3.2}{-3} and \lr{4}{-3} in Joint; Large adapt had been run with the Joint pair's rate and was retuned on its own, which moved its test-stream average from 50.2\% to 56.2\% and Small adapt from 46.7\% to 50.5\% (plan.md, 2026-10-24).

\subsection{Joint (static duo), 02b}
Both members adapt on the same combined entropy, so their rates interact and are searched as a \emph{pair}: a \(\times2\) ladder of pairs from the single rates upward, a \(\sqrt2\) refinement around the best, the leading pairs on three seeds, with the late-stream guard against the frozen duo. The gate weights are all \(\tfrac12\): this tunes Joint at \(\beta=0\), not Joint DD. The result is far from the single rates (main duo \lr{3.2}{-3}, \lr{4}{-3}, i.e. 46 and 8 times them).

\subsection{Joint DD: rates and gate together, 02f}\label{sec:jointdd}
In Joint DD the gate is part of the loss: the weights \(w_L, w_S\) decide how much each member's predictions count in the entropy both members minimize. \(\beta\) therefore changes what each member learns, so a rate tuned without the gate (02b) is not the rate that is best with it, and the best \(\beta\) depends on the rates. Tuning rates at \(\beta=0\) and then choosing \(\beta\) (what 02b and script 09 did) cannot see this. 02f searches a \emph{cell} \((\text{lr}_L, \text{lr}_S, \beta)\) with one live run per cell (a gated run cannot be replayed from stored logits), over the four tuning shifts, in a \emph{nested} way: at every \(\beta\) of \(\{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1\}\) the pair is searched on the \(\sqrt2\) grid anchored at the recorded Joint pair, then the betas are compared at their \emph{own} best pairs, ties going to the lowest \(\beta\) (so a choice of 0 means the gate does not help even with the rates retuned). A report-only confirmation on three seeds compares the recorded pair, the best pair without the gate and the chosen pair.

\emph{Why nested.} A coordinate search (pair at one \(\beta\), then \(\beta\), as the reset tuning does) cannot follow a ridge on which the best pair moves with \(\beta\); a synthetic ridge of that shape stayed stuck at \(\beta=0.5\) in the first draft's test.

\emph{What it found (main duo, Table~\ref{tab:betapair}).} The best \vit{} rate falls as \(\beta\) rises, \lr{3.2}{-3} (\(\beta=0\)), \lr{1.6}{-3}, \lr{8}{-4}, \lr{8}{-4}, \lr{5.7}{-4} (\(\beta=1\)), while the \rn{}'s stays near \lr{3}{-3}--\lr{4}{-3}; and the pair tuned without the gate \((3.2{\cdot}10^{-3}, 4{\cdot}10^{-3})\) \emph{drifts} (late accuracy below the frozen duo's) at every \(\beta>0\), scoring 53--56\% where the retuned pair scores 59.9--61.3\%. Retuning is therefore not optional once the gate is on. The gate itself did not help on these shifts: \(\beta=0\) is best (61.9\%), and the chosen pair (\lr{2.26}{-3}, \lr{2.83}{-3}) ties the recorded one (61.92 against 61.94 on three seeds, paired difference \(-0.03\pm0.01\)). The search ran 99 cells on seed 0 plus the confirmation runs. It was \textbf{not recorded}: the Joint file still holds the 02b pair, which script 03, script 09 and the reset tuning read. It has \textbf{not been run for \vit{} + \cnx{}}, whose Joint rates are the \(\beta=0\) pair.

\subsection{The Vanilla DD gate, 07 and 08}
The gate is outside the loss, so it does not change the members: they are tuned alone (the independent pair) and the gate is chosen \emph{afterwards}, offline, from the stored logits: every proxy, \(\beta\) and proxy batch size is evaluated on the tuning shifts, the winner is the cell with \(\beta>0\) of best macro accuracy (ties to the proxy with the higher ordering accuracy). The gate of all reset experiments is the nuclear-norm proxy, proxy batch 256, \(\beta=0.5\) (\(\beta\) fixed by decision for the final comparison; it affects only the evaluation of Vanilla DD, not the members). This asymmetry, gate outside against inside the loss, is exactly why Vanilla DD and Joint DD are tuned differently: the first is two independent rate problems plus an offline gate choice, the second is one coupled problem.

\subsection{Continual runs and resets}\label{sec:reset}
A rate chosen from source weights at every shift (episodic) can collapse a member on a long stream, and a rate chosen without resets is wrong once a reset rule is running, because collapses are then caught and larger rates become usable. The continual setting is therefore tuned separately (\rl{docs/reset\_tuning.md}, \rl{scripts/tune\_reset.py}), on the tuning shifts concatenated (4 shifts \(\times\) 4 rounds, 800,000 images, one rotation), in stages:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Continual ladder (04b).} Each member's rate on a long \(\sqrt2\) ladder with no reset: the \emph{frontier} (largest stable rate), the \emph{conservative} rate (one rung below it) and the \emph{stress} rate (lowest that dies completely). They are used as healthy and collapsing streams to calibrate detectors, and as the no-reset baselines.
  \item \textbf{Detector screen (offline).} For the rule \rl{own\_fast\_vs\_slow} (a member is reset when a fast moving average of its proxy falls more than \(\delta\) noise units below a slow one) a grid of settings is replayed against labelled degradation, keeping those within a false-alarm budget with the smallest delay.
  \item \textbf{Search (live runs).} Independent signal: each member's rate and detector setting, on the member's own accuracy. Joint signal: the \emph{pair}, \(\beta\) and each member's detector together, scored on the duo (the same coupling as 02f).
  \item \textbf{Baselines tuned the same way:} no reset, a reset every 500 and every 1,000 steps, each with its rates tuned with the schedule running.
\end{enumerate}
The effect on the rates is large (Table~\ref{tab:borrow}): the main duo's \vit{} goes from \lr{7}{-5} (episodic) to \lr{1.26}{-5} (no reset, conservative) to \lr{5.6}{-4} (with its reset rule).

\section{Putting it together: the order, and why rates are not borrowed}\label{sec:borrow}
The dependencies fix an order: temperatures \(\rightarrow\) single rates \(\rightarrow\) independent pair \(\rightarrow\) Large/Small adapt and Joint (anchored on the single rates) \(\rightarrow\) Joint DD with the gate (anchored on the Joint pair) \(\rightarrow\) the Vanilla DD gate (at the independent pair) \(\rightarrow\) the continual ladder \(\rightarrow\) reset schedules, rules and detector settings.

Table~\ref{tab:borrow} puts the same member's recorded rate under each regime, for the two duos of Part~II. A member's rate spans between one and two orders of magnitude depending on the regime: the main duo's \vit{} runs from \lr{1.26}{-5} to \lr{3.2}{-3} (250 times), its \rn{} from \lr{2.2}{-5} to \lr{4}{-3}. Using one regime's rate in another is not a small error: it is the difference between a healthy run and a drift (the pair tuned without the gate drifts as soon as the gate is on, Table~\ref{tab:betapair}), or between 50.2\% and 56.2\% (Large adapt, above). Each regime therefore needs its own tuning run, on its own signal, with the rule of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}.

\section{Why the duo picks a smaller \cnx{} rate than \cnx{} alone}\label{sec:whypair}
In the independent pair each member adapts on its own entropy, so at a given rate the \cnx{} is \emph{the same model} as when it runs alone: 02c scores both regimes from the same stored member logits. Only the score differs: the member's own accuracy (single) or the static duo's (independent pair). One might expect a rate that is best for the member to be best for the duo too. That holds only if the duo's accuracy rises with each member's own accuracy, and here it does not (Table~\ref{tab:whypair}).

\paragraph{Gains are attenuated and losses amplified in the duo.} Going from the pair's rate (\lr{3.1}{-6}) to the single rate (\lr{3.5}{-5}), the member alone gains 7.2 points on speckle, 6.6 on spatter and 2.7 on saturate, and loses 15.8 on Gaussian blur: \(+0.1\) on average. The duo gains only 2.7, 4.2 and 1.4 on the first three (the \vit{} already gets many of the images the \cnx{} learns to classify), and loses 21.5 on blur, \emph{more} than the member itself: \(-3.3\) on average. The criterion is the same in both regimes; the duo's objective is simply not monotone in the member's accuracy.

\paragraph{Why the duo loses more than the member: the collapsed member is confident.} Table~\ref{tab:mech} looks inside the blur stream (seed 0, stored logits). At \lr{3.5}{-5} Tent has driven the \cnx{} to a near-constant prediction: one class takes 90\% of its predictions, with a mean confidence of 0.94 \emph{on the images it gets wrong}. The duo then follows the \cnx{} on 82\% of the images and overrides a correct \vit{} on 22.6\% of them, which brings the duo (11.4) far below the \vit{} alone (33.4). A wrong but uncertain member would barely move the average; a confidently wrong one takes it over. The member's own accuracy cannot register the difference, the duo's can.

\paragraph{What this means.} (i) A member's best rate inside a duo is a different quantity from its best rate alone, even when the members never see each other's loss: the duo rewards a member for being right where its partner is wrong and penalizes it heavily for being confidently wrong, and a member's own accuracy measures neither. (ii) The duo's score is in effect a risk-averse criterion for each member: a rate that wins on average by trading one shift for three others loses in the duo. (iii) The two regimes disagree less than the \(\times 11\) suggests. The single-model optimum sits on a flat average (48.6--49.3 over \(2.2{\cdot}10^{-6}\)--\(3.5{\cdot}10^{-5}\)) and wins by \(0.1\) point because the mean accepts the blur collapse; under the \rl{worst\_shift} criterion the single-model tuner picks \lr{2.2}{-6}, one grid step from the pair's \lr{3.1}{-6}. (iv) On the main duo the two regimes coincide (\lr{7}{-5}, \lr{5}{-4}): \rn{}'s best rate alone does not buy its average with a collapse on one shift (worst shift 22.3\% at \lr{5}{-4}, 22.4\% one step below), so there is no trade for the duo to refuse.

\section{What is happening}\label{sec:what}
\paragraph{1. The selection is blind to single-shift collapses.} The criterion averages over four shifts, and the guard compares an average with an average. For \cnx{} a rate can gain 22, 14 and 5 points on three shifts and lose 18 on the fourth and still win (Table~\ref{tab:own_held}). The optimum sits next to a cliff (the next rate up breaks spatter, and its seeds disagree), so a small distribution change moves the model over it.

\paragraph{2. The tuning shifts are not representative of the failures.} Speckle noise, Gaussian blur, spatter and saturate contain no fog, snow, frost, elastic transform or contrast shift, and these are where the test collapses are (Tables~\ref{tab:own_test}, \ref{tab:test_corr}). The tuning shifts can only reveal collapses of the kind they contain (Gaussian blur, and spatter at larger rates).

\paragraph{3. The same pattern in the other new duo, but not in the main duo.} Table~\ref{tab:duos} compares the three duos (plan.md, 2026-10-22, and this report): with the main duo (\vit{} + ResNet-50) the tuned rates transfer and Joint is +13 over the frozen duo; with ConvNeXt-B + DeiT-S the gain is small; with \vit{} + \cnx{} Joint is below frozen. (For ConvNeXt-B + DeiT-S the reset rule also loses to plain schedules on the test stream, plan.md, 2026-10-31.)

\paragraph{What this report does not establish.} (i) Why ConvNeXt members collapse under Tent: no ablation was run (the step size, momentum, the choice of LayerNorm parameters and the batch size are untested); it may be specific to Tent on this architecture. (ii) Whether the failure is a property of the duo-entropy loss: Small adapt and Joint collapse on contrast, snow and zoom blur, but the \cnx{} also collapses on its own entropy, so the duo entropy shifts the failures rather than removing them. (iii) Behaviour at other rates on the test stream: only the two own-entropy rates and the recorded rates of each mode were run on the test corruptions. (iv) Any claim about the gate or the reset rules for this duo: none was run on it.

\section{Open decisions}\label{sec:open}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Report the rows as they are,} as the case where tuning on a few shifts selects a rate beside a collapse. This needs no new runs.
  \item \textbf{Adopt a selection rule that sees single-shift collapses,} for example the highest worst-shift accuracy among rates whose average is within a tolerance of the best, or a rate one grid step below the winner. On the tuning shifts this would choose \(2.2{\cdot}10^{-6}\) or \(3.1{\cdot}10^{-6}\) for \cnx{} on its own entropy (about frozen quality) and \(5{\cdot}10^{-5}\) for Small adapt. It would have to be applied to every duo, including the main one, and the tuning rerun. Whether it would have avoided the test collapses is not known: they are on corruptions the tuning shifts do not contain. \emph{Decided 3 November} (\rl{DD\_final\_sprint.md}, D7): \rl{worst\_shift} with a 1-point tolerance for this duo, in every tuning and test; the main duo keeps the mean, where the rule would change only the independent pair's \vit{} rate (\lr{7}{-5} to \lr{10}{-4}: mean \(-0.03\), worst shift \(+1.1\)).
  \item \textbf{Widen the tuning shifts} (add a weather and a digital shift), which changes the protocol for every duo.
\end{enumerate}
